# Worker Privacy Notice — TEMPLATE, DRAFT

> **DRAFT — NOT REVIEWED BY A SOLICITOR. DO NOT PUBLISH.**
> Version 0.1 draft, 16 September 2026. See `docs/legal/README.md`.

**This is a template for our customers to give to their workers.** The employer
is the controller; this is their notice, not ours. They have to fill in the
bracketed parts and, more importantly, decide whether what it says is actually
true of how they intend to use the product.

It is written to be read by somebody standing in a van at seven in the morning,
which is why it is in plain words and short sentences. **A notice nobody reads
has not informed anybody**, and "we told them, it was in the handbook" is not a
defence that survives contact with the ICO.

Three things the employer must do before handing this out:

1. **Fill in every `[...]`.**
2. **Decide the lawful basis honestly.** It is drafted as legitimate interests,
   which is usually right for an employer and is almost always better than
   consent — consent given by an employee to their employer is rarely free, and
   a consent that can be withdrawn leaves you with no basis at all the moment
   somebody withdraws it. If you go with legitimate interests you need a
   **Legitimate Interests Assessment** on file.
3. **Consult.** Where a union or employee representatives exist, consult them
   before rolling out location tracking. Where they do not, give people a real
   chance to ask questions. The ICO's employment practices guidance expects it.

Delete this box before giving the notice to anybody.

# How [COMPANY NAME] records your working time

**[COMPANY NAME]** uses an app called Vantro to record when and where you start
and finish work. This tells you what is recorded, why, who sees it, and what you
can do about it.

## What is recorded

**When you sign in and out.** The time, to the minute.

**Where you were when you did it.** Your location at the moment you sign in and
at the moment you sign out. The app checks whether you were at the job site and
records how far away you were if you were not.

**Where you are while you are signed in.** This is the part people are usually
surprised by, so it is written out plainly:

- The app watches whether you go more than **150 metres** from the job site, and
  records your position when you leave that circle and when you come back.
- It also records your position **at most once an hour** while you are signed
  in.

> **It stops when your shift does.** Recording runs from when you sign in until
> you sign out, or until your expected finish time, whichever comes first.
> Nothing is recorded before your shift, after it, on the way home, at the
> weekend, or on a day you did not sign in. If you are not signed in, the app is
> not recording where you are.

These during-shift positions are kept for **90 days** and then deleted, whatever
retention policy the company has chosen for everything else.

**Your hours and your pay.** Hours worked, breaks, lateness against your
schedule, overtime, and what you are owed.

**What you put in the app.** Photographs you take, notes and diary entries you
write, incident and defect reports, signatures, and any video or voice
recordings you make on a site walkthrough.

**Your phone.** Which version of the app you are running, and a token so
notifications reach you.

## Why

| Why | What it covers |
|---|---|
| To pay you correctly | Hours, breaks, overtime, rates |
| To know who is on site | Safety — in an emergency, who needs to be accounted for |
| To prove the work happened | Evidence for the client and for disputes about what was done and when |
| Health and safety records | Incidents, method statements, equipment checks |
| To meet our legal obligations | Working time records, tax, health and safety law |

**The legal basis** is our legitimate interests in running the business, paying
people accurately and keeping sites safe, and our legal obligations as an
employer. **[EMPLOYER: amend if you have concluded differently. Do not write
"consent" unless you genuinely mean it and can live with it being withdrawn.]**

You can ask us for our assessment of why those interests do not override your
rights. We have written it down. **[EMPLOYER: have you? Write the LIA.]**

## Who sees it

- **Your supervisor and the office** — your hours, whether you were on site, and
  what you recorded.
- **Payroll** — your hours and pay.
- **The client, sometimes** — where a compliance pack or evidence pack is issued
  for a job, which can include your name and the records you made.
- **Vantro**, the company that makes the app, which stores it for us and cannot
  use it for anything else.
- **Nobody else**, unless the law requires it.

Your location is **not** shown to your colleagues.

## Where it goes

The database and the file storage are in the **European Union**.

Some of it goes to the United States: emails we send, text you write that is
automatically summarised, addresses looked up for mapping, and notifications to
your phone. These transfers are covered by the legal safeguards UK law requires.

## Your rights

You can:

- **Ask for a copy** of everything we hold about you.
- **Ask us to correct it** if it is wrong. If the app has your hours wrong, tell
  your supervisor first — it is usually quicker.
- **Ask us to delete it**, although see below.
- **Object** to us using it for our legitimate interests, and we have to stop
  unless we can show compelling grounds not to.
- **Ask for it in a portable format.**

Contact **[NAME, ROLE, EMAIL]**. We will answer within one month and we will not
charge you.

### What deletion cannot do

If you ask us to delete your records, we will — but three things stay, and you
should know before you ask:

1. **Compliance packs already sent to a client** keep the name that was in them.
   They are sealed documents; changing one would destroy the thing that makes it
   trustworthy.
2. **Shift, safety and quality records stay, with your name taken off.** They
   are health and safety evidence, and the law specifically says that kind of
   record does not have to be erased. What survives is the record, not you: your
   name is replaced by a code.
3. **Backups** are overwritten on their own schedule rather than edited.

### If you are not happy

Speak to **[NAME]** first. If that does not resolve it, you can complain to the
**Information Commissioner's Office** — ico.org.uk, 0303 123 1113. You can do
that at any time and you do not need our permission.

### Open questions for review

1. **The lateness question.** The product records minutes late against a
   schedule, and employers will use it in disciplinary processes. The template
   makes the employer say so. A solicitor should confirm that is the right
   approach rather than a liability.
2. **Location refusal.** The template makes the employer state the consequence.
   Whether an employer can require location as a condition of working needs
   proper advice, and it is the question most likely to reach a tribunal.
3. **Evidence packs reaching clients** with worker names in them is a
   disclosure workers do not expect. It is called out; confirm the wording is
   enough.
4. **Whether to offer this as a filled-in PDF from within the product.** A
   template that requires ten decisions will be handed out unedited by somebody.
   Generating it from the company's actual settings — their retention choice,
   their DPO contact, whether background location is on — would remove most of
   the ways it can be wrong. That is a product change, not a drafting one.
